Known gaps (Phase 7): the generated trips exercised only one infeasibility type (a must-visit with unknown hours), so the independent audit of other conflict codes rests on the hand-built tests, not the 60 trips; 60 trips and 12 attractions are a small sample with no significance testing; the nearest-neighbour baseline has no must-visit priority (a stricter human would add one); the free-tier CPU may change solver statuses; explanation and parsing quality are not measured (needs the opt-in live evaluation). Known gaps (Phase 8/final hardening): cancelling or leaving a request in the browser does not stop a solve already running on the server; the solver capacity gate is per process rather than distributed across replicas; the CSP needs `style-src 'unsafe-inline'` (Leaflet and Vue inline styles; scripts stay `'self'`); Lighthouse scores are from a local run, not the live site; the remaining local Lighthouse accessibility warning is Leaflet marker target spacing, with the timeline/list kept as the accessible equivalent.

Known gaps (Phase 9b M1): Singapore and Seoul facts are sample/general-knowledge placeholders and have not been verified; non-Tokyo evaluation samples are small; legacy result field names still use `jpy` even when the city's local currency is SGD or KRW, with explicit city/currency metadata added for honest display; the frontend does not yet expose city switching until Milestone 2.

Known limits (Phase 9c): detecting an unsupported city depends on the AI reporting it; with no AI, names that aren't in the selected city's data are reported as not found. The destination check recognises only the four supported cities' names (and their areas, attractions and hotels) by itself.
